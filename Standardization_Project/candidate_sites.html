Regional Housing Planning

Project Overview

I developed standardized GIS datasets for Housing Element candidate sites across 35 Southern California jurisdictions. The project required reviewing publicly available planning documents, extracting parcel information, reconciling assessor parcel numbers from different years, cleaning inconsistent attributes, and producing GIS-ready spatial datasets.

The completed datasets were delivered to the Southern California Association of Governments for regional mapping products.

The Challenge

Turning Inconsistent Documents Into Usable GIS Data

Candidate site information was distributed across Housing Element documents containing hundreds of pages. Each jurisdiction presented its information differently, including variations in table layouts, field names, parcel identifiers, site descriptions, and reporting formats.

Inconsistent Formats

Candidate site tables were structured differently across jurisdictions and required manual interpretation before the information could be standardized.

Changing Parcel Numbers

APNs referenced in planning documents did not always match current parcel datasets because parcels had been split, combined, renumbered, or recorded during different assessment years.

Missing or Uneven Attributes

Some documents contained incomplete or differently named fields, requiring careful validation and consistent categorization.

Regional Consistency

Data from all 35 jurisdictions needed to follow a common structure before it could support regional analysis and mapping.

Technical Workflow

From Planning Documents to Regional GIS Data

Quality Assurance and Quality Control

Validation Process

Because the project combined data from multiple documents, jurisdictions, and parcel years, QA/QC was central to the workflow rather than something performed only at the end.

Attribute Validation

Checked required fields, naming conventions, data types, null values, duplicate site identifiers, and inconsistent classifications.

APN Validation

Removed formatting differences and investigated unmatched, outdated, split, combined, or renumbered parcels.

Spatial Validation

Confirmed that candidate sites matched the correct parcel geometry and jurisdiction boundaries.
